The brief

Built by students who love to compete.

Having been fortunate to participate in coding competitions locally and internationally, we experienced the power of community in code. But we noticed something missing: a local platform for schools to engage in computer science together. So we built one.

TigerHacks brings students from multiple schools together to celebrate innovation, problem‑solving, and creativity—through a friendly coding competition designed by high‑schoolers, for high‑schoolers.

How it works

Read the evidence. Crack the case.

Teams race the clock to solve a set of algorithmic problems. Every submission is judged automatically against hidden tests — only complete, exact solutions count.

01

Graded on HackerRank

Submit solutions in your language of choice. Grading runs automatically.

C++JavaPython
02

Hidden tests, no partial credit

Example I/O is provided, but you must pass every hidden test — and match the output exactly, whitespace and newlines included.

03

Speed breaks ties

When scores are level, the time taken to solve problems decides the ranking. Manage your time carefully.

Interrogation room

Questions, answered.

Everything you need to know before competition day.

When is TigerHacks 2026?

The date for TigerHacks 2026 has not been announced yet. It will be posted on this page as soon as it is confirmed.

Where is the competition held?

TigerHacks takes place at 257 Stanwich Road, Greenwich, CT 06830, hosted by Greenwich Country Day School.

Which programming languages can we use?

Submissions and grading run on HackerRank. Accepted languages are C++, Java, and Python.

What is the theme for TigerHacks 2026?

TigerHacks 2026 is murder-mystery themed. Expect a case to crack, clues to follow, and a culprit to unmask.

What resources are allowed during the competition?

Official language documentation and general tutorial sites are permitted. Q&A forums such as StackOverflow are not, and no outside assistance is allowed from anyone not authorized to help — including other teams and chaperones.

How is the competition scored?

Example input/output cases are provided, but grading uses hidden tests. A solution must pass all tests to count — there is no partial credit — and output must match exactly, including formatting, whitespace, and newlines. Ties are broken by the time taken to solve problems.

How many laptops can a team bring?

One laptop per team.

How do we contact the organizers?

Email the TigerHacks team at tigerhacks@gcds.net.

How can we prepare?

Work through the problem sets from 2023, 2024, and 2025 — solutions for each year are linked alongside them.
